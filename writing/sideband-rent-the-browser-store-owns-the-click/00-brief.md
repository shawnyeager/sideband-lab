# Locked brief — Sideband essay (Shawn Yeager, 27 Sep 2026)

Verbatim copy of the Shawn-approved brief. Governs every stage below.

## Claim (Shawn confirmed)
Consumers will keep pushing agents (Muse, Grok Bot) for convenience; retailers cite fraud and rogue-agent risk until that excuse stops holding; obstinate stores lose share to stores that embrace it; the fight is an arms race (Amazon wall, Walmart CAPTCHA, Browser Use bypass), and the piece opens that who-wins conversation without clever rent-tax theater.

## Stake / who wins
Retailers that stay obstinate lose market share. Retailers that embrace agent convenience win.

## Opposing case (Shawn)
Retailers must combat fraud and protect consumers from rogue agents — and that case is real — but there comes a point when that will no longer hold.

## Stay
- Amazon Muse denial as one notable receipt (biggest online retailer) — SHORT, not the whole spine.
- Shawn personal: tried to get Grok Bot to shop Walmart; aggressive CAPTCHAs stopped it; workaround was install Browser Use, which got around even intense captures. Point: arms race.
- Other researched/validated examples (do not invent). Research pack attached.
- Clear, not clever. No unnecessary theatrics.

## Die
- "You rent the browser / store owns the click" rent-tax slogan spine
- Tools-list / meter parade / vendor leaderboard
- Clever theatrics, slogan-triad dek
- Kauffman naming
- Em dashes; "hire/hired" for software (users *use* software)
- Anthropomorphism of agents or animated objects
- Hollow title-echo closes; "All of that can be true" theater
- Invented quotes/numbers

## Outline (Shawn approved)
1. Cold open: Amazon Muse popup as one big receipt (short), then Walmart CAPTCHA / Browser Use bypass as lived arms-race beat.
2. Plain claim: consumers push agent convenience; retailers cite fraud/rogue agents until that stops holding; obstinate lose share, embracers win.
3. Receipts braid (validated only): second retailer or CDN wall; one signed-lane or stealth-meter beat *only if* it earns the arms-race point; no tools-list parade.
4. Retailers' best case at full force (fraud, rogue agents), then the cut: that excuse has a shelf life.
5. Land: who wins / who loses — clear, not clever. No rent-tax slogan spine.
