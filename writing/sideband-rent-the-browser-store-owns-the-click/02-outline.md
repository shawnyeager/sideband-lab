## Section: The claim (beat 2), ~90 words

**Main point:** Plain statement of Shawn's claim.

**Transition:** from "arms race" to "where it ends."

**Beats:**
- Shoppers will keep reaching for agents because the agent finishes the cart.
- Retailers will keep citing fraud and rogue agents, and today that case is real.
- It stops holding at some point. After that, stores that keep saying no lose share to stores that say yes.

**Bottom line:** one sentence Shawn could text: "Stores that keep blocking shopping agents on fraud grounds will lose share to the ones that let them in, once the fraud argument stops holding."

## Section: The receipts braid (beat 3), ~150 words

**Main point:** Both sides are escalating in public, and the escalation points toward identity.

**Transition:** "The wall is already in writing."

**Beats:**
- Wall in contract: Amazon's Agent Terms (updated 14 Aug 2026) bar agents from "completing or circumventing CAPTCHAs" and from mimicking human keystrokes (R2). The Walmart workaround is exactly the move Amazon wrote a rule against.
- Signed lane (the one allowed beat): Cloudflare's signed agents, Aug 2025 (R7). The remote browser signs each HTTP request and Cloudflare validates it; ChatGPT agent was in the first cohort. The site still decides; it no longer has to guess.
- Amazon's own rule asks for the same thing in cruder form: `Agent/[agent name]` in every request (R2). Muse didn't send it (R1).

**Supporting material:** R2 quote; R7 mechanism in one sentence. No vendor list, no pricing.

**Bottom line:** the arms race has an exit, and it is identification.

---

## Section: The retailers' case, then the cut (beat 4), ~150 words

**Main point:** Fraud and rogue-agent risk are real today. Once identity and payment scoping exist, that argument expires.

**Transition:** "Take the retailers' side at full force."

**Beats:**
- Full force: an agent logging in with a customer's stored password is an undisclosed third party inside the account; Amazon says Muse can reach account pages and order history (R1). To a fraud team that looks like account takeover. Stores that open the door eat the loss when one goes rogue.
- The cut: the pieces that answer it already ship. Muse pays with a single-use card scoped to the approved purchase; the buyer approves every total (R6). Signing tells the site who is knocking (R7).
- Amazon asked for more than identification. It asked Meta to remove Amazon from Muse (R1). Candidate money line: $68B ad business that depends on people browsing its pages (R1).
- Candidate control line: Walmart runs its own Sparky agent inside ChatGPT (R9). The walls are aimed at agents the store does not run.

**Bottom line:** once fraud stops being the reason, what remains is control of the customer.

## Conclusion: who wins (beat 5), ~110 words

**Main point:** Stores that embrace agent checkout win share. Stores that hold out pay for the arms race and lose the order.

**Transition:** the day after Amazon's popup.

**Beats:**
- Mon 21 Sep: Tobi Lütke announces Shop Pay checkout for Muse "on all Shopify stores" (R8). Checkout on by default; merchant stays merchant of record (R8).
- Who can afford to hold out: Amazon, for a while. A mid-size store whose Shopify competitor takes the approved order cannot.
- Losers: stores answering with CAPTCHAs against tools any shopper can install.

**Kicker:** end on the concrete consequence (where the order goes), not an echo of the title and not a slogan.
